Abstract (draft)

Multimodal EEG / eye-tracking screening for paediatric psychiatric status has reported high accuracies, but frequently under evaluation schemes vulnerable to information leakage. We present NeuroFormer, a unified architecture that ingests one-to-three asynchronous modalities through modality-specific encoders and a confidence-gated fusion with an explicit, mask-based missing-modality mechanism; and — as our central methodological contribution — a standardized, leakage-free, confound-controlled evaluation protocol applied identically to NeuroFormer and 13 comparator models across two cohorts. On a rigorously controlled paediatric cohort (N = 92), subject-level cross-validation, 1000-permutation testing, per-model bootstrap confidence intervals, and DeLong comparisons show that no model — including NeuroFormer — exceeds a two-variable age+sex baseline (AUC 0.737), while window-level leakage inflates AUC to 0.95–1.00 versus ≈0.51 honest; covariate-matched subsampling and confound-conditioned permutation attribute the residual signal largely to demographics. On an independent adult cohort (ds003478, EEG-only), by contrast, EEG genuinely predicts depression (AUC ≈ 0.75), exceeds demographics, and survives age/sex residualization and within-stratum permutation (p ≈ 0.001). The missing-modality mechanism is shown to remain operational under dropped modalities. Our contribution is the unified architecture together with an honest, reproducible characterization of what multimodal EEG screening can and cannot deliver at realistic sample sizes.

Contributions

  1. NeuroFormer, a unified architecture supporting one-to-three asynchronous modalities via modality-specific encoders and confidence-gated fusion, with a demonstrated (implemented and evaluated) mask-based missing-modality mechanism.
  2. A standardized, leakage-free, confound-controlled evaluation protocol — subject-level CV, LOSO, nested CV, bootstrap, 1000-permutation testing, a demographic baseline, DeLong tests, and calibration — applied identically to all 14 models on identical inputs.
  3. An honest two-cohort characterization: on a controlled paediatric cohort no model beats a two-variable demographic baseline and prior high accuracies are shown to be leakage/confound artifacts; on an independent cohort where the confound is weak, EEG-based signal is genuine. We make no claim of accuracy superiority.

Part A — Methods

A.0 Overview and contributions

We study multimodal (EEG + eye-tracking) screening of pooled paediatric psychiatric status. Our contributions are: (i) NeuroFormer, a unified architecture that ingests one-to-three asynchronous modalities through modality-specific encoders and a confidence-gated fusion, including a masked missing-modality mechanism; (ii) a standardized, leakage-free, confound-controlled evaluation protocol applied identically to NeuroFormer and 13 comparator models; and (iii) an honest two-cohort characterization of what such screening can and cannot deliver at realistic sample sizes. We make no claim of accuracy superiority; the value is the architecture together with the rigorous evaluation.

A.1 Datasets and cohorts

  • MIPDB (primary, within-cohort). Resting-state EEG with concurrent eye-tracking. Binary label DX_Status (diagnosed vs control). After requiring both EEG and eye modalities present (Section A.4), the locked analysis cohort is N = 92 (20 positive, 72 control). The locked subject list is versioned (cohort_final.txt).
  • ds003478 (external replication). Cavanagh resting-state EEG (adults), EEG-only; depression label from BDI (≥ 14 = depressed; extreme-groups ≤ 7 vs ≥ 14 as sensitivity). N ≈ 121 (45 depressed). No eye-tracking or behavioural modality exists in this dataset, so multimodal-fusion models are reported as N/A there rather than run degenerately.

Two cohorts serve complementary roles: MIPDB for within-cohort rigor under a demographic confound; ds003478 for external replication in a cohort where that confound is weak.

A.2 Signal acquisition and preprocessing (EEG)

Identical pipeline for both cohorts. Continuous EEG is (i) notch-filtered at 60 Hz, (ii) band-pass filtered 1–45 Hz (4th-order Butterworth), and (iii) cleaned with ICA for ocular/muscular/cardiac artifact removal (Winkler et al., 2011). Cleaned signal is segmented into non-overlapping 15 s windows.

A.3 Feature extraction

  • EEG (per window, per channel — 15 features): three Hjorth parameters (activity, mobility, complexity), spectral entropy, five absolute band powers, five relative band powers, and the theta/beta ratio. Bands: delta (1–4 Hz), theta (4–8), alpha (8–13), beta (13–30), gamma (30–45). MIPDB uses a 111-channel EGI scalp montage (channels E1–E111) → 1665 EEG features/window (111 × 15); ds003478 has fewer channels (feature length truncated to the common minimum across subjects to preserve channel-contiguous ordering).
  • Eye-tracking (per subject): oculomotor summary features — pupil-size variability, mean/SD horizontal and vertical gaze position, head-position variability, saccade latency (mean/SD), and gaze-vector magnitude (mean/SD).
  • Behaviour: not available for the MIPDB analysis cohort (empty stream); the architecture supports it but it is not used here, and no behavioural result is reported.

A.4 Modality assembly and dimensionality

Two feature representations are derived per subject: a subject-level vector (mean over a subject's EEG windows, concatenated with the eye vector) for the static models, and a per-window EEG sequence for the temporal models. For numerical stability at small N, EEG features are reduced to the top-128 highest-variance dimensions (variance computed on training folds' convention; fixed index set per cohort). All features are standardized with a StandardScaler fit on the training fold only (no test leakage). The locked cohort is the intersection of EEG-present and eye-present subjects (N = 92).

Uniform inputs across models (and comparability note). Every model — NeuroFormer included — receives the same 128-dimensional handcrafted EEG (+ eye) representation, so all 14 methods are compared on identical inputs (a fairness constraint of the protocol). Note this differs from the original NeuroFormer implementation, which consumed a 516-dimensional processed EEG representation plus a behavioural stream (three modalities); NeuroFormer's score under the present standardized protocol is therefore not directly comparable to that original number, and we report it only as one entry in this uniform benchmark.

A.5 Models — a 14-model comparator suite

All models predict the binary label and are evaluated under the identical protocol (Section A.7). Class imbalance is handled with balanced class weights / positive-class loss weighting throughout.

Static feature-space (subject-level concat): Logistic Regression, RBF-SVM, Random Forest, MLP (early fusion), Late-fusion ensemble. Temporal (per-window EEG sequences): 1D-CNN, LSTM, TCN. Multimodal fusion: Gated Multimodal Unit (GMU; Arévalo 2017), Cross-modal attention Transformer, Tensor Fusion Network (Zadeh 2017), Low-rank Multimodal Fusion (Liu 2018). Proposed: NeuroFormer (Section A.6). Null baseline: Demographics — logistic regression on age and sex only (no EEG, no eye). This is the reference every model must clear.

A.6 NeuroFormer architecture (input → output)

NeuroFormer is a two-stage, modality-specific encoder with confidence-gated fusion.

  1. EEG encoder (hierarchical). Per-sample Euclidean alignment (channel-wise standardization) → an EEGNet-style tokenizer (temporal convolution + depthwise-separable spatial convolution, batch-norm, ReLU) → criss-cross attention (parallel spatial and temporal multi-head self-attention, summed) → frequency-aware attention (the hidden representation is split into four band sub-spaces, each refined by band-specific attention, then recombined by a learned band-fusion weighting) → a residual MLP. Hidden width 64.
  2. Eye/behaviour encoders. Linear projection + ReLU + dropout to the shared 64-d space.
  3. Confidence-gated fusion. Each modality emits a scalar confidence (Linear → sigmoid); a softmax over confidences produces fusion gates; the fused representation is the confidence-weighted sum of modality embeddings, passed to a 2-way classifier. An optional cross-modal refinement lets the EEG embedding modulate the weaker modalities.

Missing-modality mechanism (implemented and evaluated). Availability is represented by a per-sample mask: an absent modality's confidence logit is set to −∞ (excluded from the softmax) and its features zeroed — i.e. it is masked out of the gate, not fed as zeros. The model is trained with modality dropout (each training sample randomly drops one modality with probability 0.5), so it learns to classify from any available subset. At inference the same model is tested under EEG+eye, EEG-only, and eye-only availability.

A.7 Evaluation protocol (the methodological core)

The central design choice is subject-level cross-validation: all windows/records of a subject fall entirely in train or test. We contrast this with window-level CV (windows of the same subject in both train and test), which reproduces the inflated accuracies reported in prior work on our own data and quantifies the leakage. Primary metric: ROC-AUC (discrimination), the natural quantity for "is this separable above the baseline?".

We report, for every model: - Repeated 5-fold (5 folds × 10 repeats): per-fold AUCs and their 2.5/97.5 percentile CI. - Pooled out-of-fold (OOF) AUC from a single stratified 5-fold, with a subject-level bootstrap 95% CI (2000 resamples). To guarantee cross-table consistency, OOF predictions are computed once (canonical source) and reused for the point AUC, DeLong, and calibration (Section A.10). - Leave-one-subject-out (LOSO) — the strictest subject-level check (pooled OOF AUC + CI). - Nested CV — inner grid-search for hyperparameters, outer loop for evaluation, so tuning cannot leak into the estimate (tunable models; torch nets tuned over weight-decay). - .632+ bootstrap — a small-N-appropriate estimator alongside the fold-mean and OOF. - 20-seed repeated 5-fold — the full stability distribution (100 folds), not just the mean. - Label-permutation test (1000 permutations) — per-model p-value for above-chance AUC.

A.8 Confound-control analyses

Because the diagnosed and control groups may differ demographically, we test whether classification survives controlling for age and sex: - Demographic baseline (age+sex logistic regression) as the null to clear. - Covariate-matched subsampling — repeatedly resample sex-matched and joint age+sex-matched case/control sets and re-estimate AUC (200 resamples), removing the demographic difference by design. - Confound-conditioned permutation — permute labels within demographic strata (sex; and sex × age-tertile) rather than globally, so the null preserves the confound structure. - Regress-out vs include-as-feature — compare AUC when age+sex are (a) left alone, (b) regressed out of the features (train-fit residuals), or (c) appended as extra features.

A.9 External replication (ds003478)

The full applicable model suite plus the confound and permutation analyses are re-run on ds003478 (EEG-only). This tests whether the leakage pattern (leaky ≫ honest) and the confound pattern (honest ≈ demographic baseline) generalize, and whether an above-baseline EEG signal exists in a cohort where the age/sex confound is weak.

A.10 Statistical analysis and reproducibility

Model comparisons use the DeLong test for correlated ROC-AUCs on the shared OOF predictions (pairwise, and each model vs the demographic baseline). Calibration is summarized by Brier score and Expected Calibration Error (10 bins). All randomness is seeded; SVC Platt calibration and torch operations are seeded/deterministic, and — critically — the benchmark point AUC, DeLong, and calibration all read a single canonical OOF file, so the same model has an identical AUC in every table. Code and per-model outputs are released (src/model_suite.py, drivers 17–25, results/*.csv).

Part B — Results & interpretation (what we tested, and why it is / isn't better)

We first establish, on the controlled paediatric cohort, that under leakage-free evaluation the fourteen models are statistically indistinguishable from one another and from a two-variable demographic baseline (B.1). We then explain why — decomposing the apparent signal into a demographic confound, small-sample variance, and the evaluation leakage that inflated prior reports (B.2), and showing that neither modality fusion nor the missing-modality mechanism recovers separable signal (B.3). Finally, we replicate the protocol on an independent adult cohort where the confound is weak, and there — in contrast — EEG carries genuine, demographics-independent predictive signal (B.4). Together these establish the paper's claim: not a leaderboard result, but a rigorous, two-cohort account of where multimodal EEG screening does and does not hold (B.5).

B.1 Headline: no model separates from demographics (MIPDB, N = 92)

Under leakage-free subject-level evaluation the 14 models form one overlapping band: no model's 95% CI excludes the age+sex baseline (AUC 0.737), which is in fact the single best-scoring model, and no pairwise difference is significant (DeLong). We therefore report the benchmark ordered by AUC and lead with the overlap, not the rank.

B.2 Why it is not "better": the signal is largely a demographic confound

Three independent lines converge: - Matched subsampling. Under joint age+sex matching the demographic baseline collapses from 0.71 to 0.40 and the models fall toward chance — the apparent signal was riding on age/sex.

  • Residualization. Regressing age+sex out of the EEG features removes the EEG advantage on MIPDB (adding age+sex as features, not removing them, is what helps).
  • Permutation. After FDR correction, five models exceed chance (Demographics, Random Forest, 1D-CNN, Tensor Fusion, Low-rank Fusion; q < 0.05); but exceeding chance ≠ exceeding demographics (none does, above), and the confound-conditioned permutation shows the effect is not independent of age/sex.

Two further reasons it cannot be "better" here: small N (20 positives → wide CIs, low power to separate models), and the fact that prior 90%+ numbers were leakage — on our data window-level (leaky) CV inflates AUC to 0.95–1.00 vs ~0.51 honest, so there was never a real accuracy headroom to win.

No fusion model exceeds EEG-alone. In the missing-modality experiment (modality-dropout training, masked inference), the model remains operational under any single modality:

We therefore report the mechanism as functional under missing modalities, not as graceful degradation from strong performance (there is no strong full-modality baseline to degrade from).

B.5 What this establishes

A rigorous, uniform, two-cohort evaluation shows that (a) apparent multimodal SOTA in this setting is driven by leakage and demographic confounds, (b) at realistic sample sizes no architecture — ours included — separates from a two-variable baseline, and (c) genuine EEG-based signal does exist where the confound is controlled. The contribution is the unified missing-modality architecture together with this honest characterization — a cautionary, reproducible benchmark rather than a leaderboard result.

B.6 Transparency and limitations

We state the following explicitly so that none of it can read as concealed; each follows directly from the study's design. - Relation to prior high accuracies. Earlier reports (including our own) of ~0.9 AUC in this setting arise from window-level evaluation, in which windows of the same subject appear in both train and test. Under identical models this inflates AUC to 0.95–1.00 versus ≈0.51 under subject-level evaluation (Table/Fig, leakage). The honest numbers supersede the earlier ones. - NeuroFormer inputs and comparability. All 14 models — NeuroFormer included — are evaluated on identical inputs (128-dim handcrafted EEG + eye, two modalities) under one protocol. This differs from the original NeuroFormer implementation (516-dim processed EEG + behaviour, three modalities); the score here is therefore not directly comparable to that earlier number, and even that earlier number (≈0.67) is below the demographic baseline (0.74). Code is released for inspection. - Cohort selection. The MIPDB analysis cohort (N = 92) is the subset of subjects with both EEG and eye modalities present. This is a complete-case criterion and a source of selection bias (a research cohort with high-quality dual recordings), which we do not claim to eliminate. - Two, not three, modalities. Behaviour is absent for this cohort; despite the architecture's one-to-three-modality design, only EEG and eye are used, and no behavioural result is reported. - Cross-cohort scope. ds003478 is an adult depression cohort, not a replication of the paediatric task; it demonstrates that EEG can carry demographics-independent signal where the confound is weak, not that the same model transfers. - Missing-modality claim. The mechanism is demonstrated to be operational under dropped modalities; because full-modality performance is itself low, we do not claim graceful degradation from strong performance. - Multiple comparisons. DeLong and permutation p-values are corrected with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR (and Bonferroni); corrected tables are in results/*_corrected.csv. After correction no pairwise model difference is significant (0/91) and no model differs from demographics (0/13); raw values are additionally provided. Correction strengthens the central claim. - Estimators. Point AUCs are pooled out-of-fold; permutation tests operate on the repeated-CV AUC (a distinct, more stable estimator) — the two differ marginally by construction and are labelled as such. - Sample size. N = 92 with 20 positives yields wide confidence intervals and limited power to separate models; we therefore lead with interval overlap and non-significance rather than rank order, and do not over-interpret point differences.

Part C — Discussion

Principal finding. Under a uniform, leakage-free, confound-controlled protocol, no architecture — from logistic regression to a purpose-built multimodal transformer — separates from a two-variable age+sex baseline on this cohort; after multiplicity correction the fourteen models form a single statistically indistinguishable band. The high accuracies previously reported in this setting are reproduced only under window-level evaluation, where windows of a subject leak across the train/test split (AUC ≈ 1.0 vs ≈ 0.5 honest). We therefore interpret prior multimodal "state-of-the-art" in this domain as, in substantial part, an artifact of evaluation design and demographic confounding rather than evidence of a learned neuro-behavioural biomarker.

Why the negative result is informative, not merely null. Three convergent analyses locate the residual signal in demographics: matched subsampling collapses it, age+sex residualization removes it, and confound-conditioned permutation shows it is not independent of age/sex. This is a positive, mechanistic explanation of why sophisticated models do not help here — the discriminative structure available at this sample size is largely demographic, and additional modality-fusion capacity has nothing further to exploit. Consistent with this, no fusion variant exceeds EEG-alone and, in the missing-modality experiment, eye-tracking contributes nothing (EEG-only ≥ EEG+eye).

The signal is real where the confound is not. On the independent ds003478 cohort, where the age/sex confound is weak, EEG predicts depression well above demographics and survives residualization and within-stratum permutation. This rules out the trivial reading that EEG is simply uninformative; rather, whether EEG-based screening appears to work depends critically on whether the evaluation controls for demographic structure — precisely the methodological point of the paper.

Contribution and positioning. We do not claim accuracy superiority for NeuroFormer; we claim (i) a unified architecture supporting one-to-three asynchronous modalities with a demonstrated mask-based missing-modality mechanism, and (ii) a rigorous evaluation protocol that, applied uniformly, yields an honest account of achievable performance. Evaluating a proposed model under the same leakage-free scrutiny as its baselines — and reporting that it does not beat a demographic null at N ≈ 90 — is, we argue, more useful to the field than another leaderboard number that would not survive the same scrutiny.

Clinical implications. These results counsel caution against deploying multimodal EEG screening on the basis of within-cohort accuracy alone. Reported performance should be benchmarked against a demographic baseline and validated out-of-distribution before any screening claim; on the present cohort a model that "achieves 0.7 AUC" is not clinically meaningful once age and sex are accounted for.

Limitations and future work. The primary limitation is statistical power: N = 92 (20 positive) yields wide intervals, and a genuine but small multimodal effect could be undetectable here — our claim is the absence of evidence for separation from demographics at this scale, not proof of no effect. The MIPDB cohort is also a complete-case, research-grade sample (selection bias), and behaviour was unavailable. Future work should (a) repeat this protocol at substantially larger N with prospective, demographically balanced sampling; (b) test the missing-modality architecture in settings where modalities are genuinely and informatively missing; and (c) extend the confound-controlled benchmark to additional cohorts to map where EEG-based screening does and does not carry demographics-independent signal.
